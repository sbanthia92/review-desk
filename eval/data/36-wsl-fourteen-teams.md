# Fourteen teams was the right call for the WSL

When the Women's Super League decided to grow from twelve clubs to fourteen, plenty of people told me the top flight was too thin to stretch any further. Five rounds into the 2026-27 season, I think the doubters owes the league an apology.

Start with the newcomers. Birmingham City and Crystal Palace came up automatically, and Charlton Athletic took the fourteenth place by beating Leicester City on penalties in a one-off play-off on 23 May 2026. That shoot-out put Charlton back in the top tier of the women's game after 16 years away. Nobody who watched it will tell you that the extra place was a mistake.

The crowds have followed. Arsenal's 1-1 draw with Manchester United on 19 September drew 39,853 to the Emirates Stadium. At the other end of the scale, only 1,274 people watched Crystal Palace beat Everton 1-0 on 6 September, but Palace play at a ground that holds about five thousand, and a small ground that is a quarter full of new supporters is still progress. There are less empty seats in this league every year. Clubs who move there women's team into the main stadium sell four times as many season tickets within two years.

The football has been better than the pessimists predicted, too. London City Lionesses beat Manchester United 3-1 in the opening round and sit third in the table. Tottenham won 7-0 at Crystal Palace on 20 September, with Victoria Pelova scoring a hat-trick. Manchester City, the defending champions, have won all five of their matches, and nobody watching them could of called the league predictable, because the sides behind them keep taking points off each other.

Things do not change; we change. The league did not become competitive by waiting for the perfect moment. It became competitive because somebody decided to let more clubs in and trusted them to catch up.

The clubs have taken the new season seriously off the pitch as well. Manchester United appointed Eva Olid as head coach on 5 August 2026, two days after Marc Skinner left, and Brighton changed their head coach a week before the first game. Owners do not make changes like that for a competition they regard as a sideshow.

I know the objection. Charlton have lost every game so far, and the gap between the top and the bottom is wide. But the answer to that is simple. Attendances are up at the biggest clubs, so the expansion has plainly worked for every club in the league. Money follows crowds, and the money will reach the bottom of the table soon enough.

The league should not stop here. Sixteen clubs is the obvious next step, and the people who said fourteen was too many should be asked what they think now. Growth was a risk, and it was the right one to take.
