# Five substitutes are handing the title to the richest squads

I have nothing against change. Football's lawmakers have got plenty of big calls right over the years, and the game is better for them. But the move to five substitutes is different, and the longer it lasts the more damage it does to the competition I love.

Start with the changes that worked. Yellow and red cards were first used at the 1970 World Cup, and they made the referee's decisions clear to everyone in the ground. The back-pass rule, introduced in 1998, stopped goalkeepers picking the ball up from a team-mate's pass and killed off the worst time-wasting the sport has ever seen. English football switched to three points for a win in 1981, and FIFA followed at the 1990 World Cup, which finally give teams a reason to chase a winner instead of settling for a draw.

Every one of those changes made the game fairer or faster. None of them gave an advantage to the clubs who already had the most.

The five-substitute rule does exactly that. A manager with eighty million pounds of talent on his bench can now replace half of his outfield players in the last half hour. A manager at a promoted club is choosing between tired legs and teenagers. Now, here, you see, it takes all the running you can do, to keep in the same place. The smaller club runs itself into the ground for an hour, and then it watch a fresh front line jog on against it.

The effect shows up in the results. Since the five-substitute rule arrived, the richest six clubs have won 12% more points from losing positions than they did before. Late goals used to be a sign of spirit. Now there a sign of squad depth, and everybody in the stadium knows it.

People defend the rule by talking about player welfare, but I do not buy it. Bigger squads benefit most from five substitutes, therefore the rule exists only to help the rich clubs. Nobody who voted for it were thinking about hamstrings.

The fix is easy. Go back to three changes, let the best eleven play, and make managers live with the team they picked on Friday afternoon. A league is only worth watching if the side at the bottom has a fair chance against the side at the top for the full ninety minutes.
