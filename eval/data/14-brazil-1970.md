# Brazil 1970 is still the best team there has ever been

I was not alive to see them, and I do not care. I have watched the surviving footage more times than is healthy, and I have reached a conclusion that no amount of modern pressing data is going to shift. The Brazil side that won the 1970 World Cup in Mexico is the best football team that has ever existed.

Consider what they actually did. They won all six of their matches at the tournament, including a group game against the reigning champions England that people still talk about because of one save by Gordon Banks. Jairzinho scored in every one of those matches. They finished the job by beating Italy 4-2 in the final at the Azteca, and the fourth goal are still the one every child tries to copy in the playground. Pelé rolled the ball into space without looking, and the captain Carlos Alberto arrived like a train to smash it into the far corner.

It was Pelé's third World Cup winner's medal and Brazil's fourth title. The win also meant that Brazil kept the Jules Rimet Trophy for good, which feels right, because nobody else was ever going to deserve it more.

The man who put the side together deserves more credit than he gets. The team was coached by Telê Santana, who somehow found room for a whole handful of playmakers in one eleven. Nobody were asked to sacrifice himself for the system. The system was built around letting great players be great, and there passing was quicker then anything the tournament had seen.

It helps that the pictures are so vivid. This was the first World Cup that most of the world watched in colour, and the yellow shirts against the Mexican sun look like something from a dream. The world outside the stadium was a mess that summer, and the football was perfect. It was the best of times, it was the worst of times, it was the age of wisdom, it was the age of foolishness, and for ninety minutes at a time none of that mattered.

The experts agree with me. In a 2020 poll, 90% of professional coaches named Brazil 1970 as the best international team in history. I have never met a former player who watched that final and came away thinking about anybody else.

I know what the modern fan will say. Today's players are fitter, the tactics are smarter, the defending is more organised. I have heard all of it, and none of it impresses me. No team since has won every match at a World Cup while playing so attractively, so the modern game has clearly gone backwards. We have swapped imagination for running, and we have been told to call it progress.

Its a standard that no team has reached since, and I do not expect to see it reached in my lifetime. Every four years a new side is described as the heirs to 1970, and every four years they turn out to be a well-drilled team with one or two stars and a good set-piece coach. That is fine. It is just not the same thing.

Some people think this is nostalgia. I think it is simply paying attention. When the best players in the world are trusted to play, and they play for each other, you get Mexico 1970. When they are not, you get a heat map.
