# Two up is not enough: give the National League a third promotion place

Anyone who still thinks the fifth tier is a backwater should have been watching it last spring. It gave us the best title race in the country, the best final at Wembley, and the clearest proof yet that the door to the Football League is too narrow.

Start with the last day of the regular season. York City finished top with 108 points, and Rochdale finished second with 104 points. The two of them met at Rochdale on 25 April 2026 with the title still open. The home side scored deep into stoppage time and their supporters poured on to the pitch, and then Josh Stones equalised with almost the last kick to send York up as champions. Rochdale's reward for all of that were a place in the play-offs.

Nothing is so painful to the human mind as a great and sudden change. Rochdale had dropped out of the Football League in 2023 after 102 years, and here they were being asked to do it all again in two matches. They beat Scunthorpe United 2-1 in the semi-final and went to Wembley to face Boreham Wood, who had finished fifth and were sixteen points behind them in the table.

The final was played in front of 21,740 people, and for more than an hour it looked like a robbery. Matt Rush and Zak Brunt put Boreham Wood two goals up. Tyler Smith pulled one back in the 78th minute, and Emmanuel Dieseruvwe headed the equaliser in the seventh minute of stoppage time. Extra time settled nothing, and Rochdale won the shoot-out 4-2. Their manager, Jimmy McNulty, looked like a man who had been told his house was not on fire after all.

It ended well, so everybody went home happy and nobody asked the obvious question. What if it had not? A team that finished second nearly stayed down, which proves that the play-offs are a lottery and should be abolished. Give the top three automatic promotion and be done with it.

The clubs know what is at stake. Teams that lose the play-off final take an average of six years to come back up. Budgets are cut, the best players leave in June, and the supporters which travelled to Wembley drift away by October.

The Football League relegated only two clubs from League Two last season, as it has every season for more than twenty years. In that time the National League has turn into a serious, mostly full-time division, with crowds that many League Two clubs would envy. Carlisle United drew 13,814 for their semi-final against Boreham Wood, and they lost it.

A third promotion place would cost the League nothing except a little comfort for it's weakest members. It would reward the clubs who run themselves properly, and it would stop a season like Rochdale's from hanging on a penalty. Two up was a fair deal for the division as it used to be. It is not a fair deal for the division as it is.
