# The women's game has outgrown its small grounds

I took my niece to her first match last season. We sat in a tidy little stadium on the edge of town with a few thousand other people, and she loved every minute of it. I spent most of the afternoon wondering why the club that owns one of the biggest grounds in the country had sent its women's team to play somewhere you need two buses to reach.

The demand is there, and it has been there for a long time. On Boxing Day in 1920 a women's works team called Dick, Kerr Ladies drew a crowd of around 53,000 to Goodison Park. The Football Association's answer, a year later, was to ban women's matches from its members' grounds. The game was not short of supporters. It was pushed out of sight by people who was embarrassed by its success.

Look at what has happened since the doors reopened. The first Women's World Cup was held in 1991 in the United States, and it has grown at every edition since. Spain won the 2023 World Cup by beating England 2-0 in Sydney, in front of a crowd that would of filled most men's finals. The Lionesses beat Germany 2-1 at Wembley in the Euro 2022 final in front of 87,192 people, which was a record for any European Championship final, men's or women's.

Those nights change lives. Girls who watch a women's match live are five times more likely to join a club. I saw it in my own family, and I have heard the same story from every coach I have spoke to at grassroots level.

So why are we still hiding the weekly product? If a man does not keep pace with his companions, perhaps it is because he hears a different drummer. The clubs who run the women's game keep pace with nobody. They wait for proof of demand, then ignore the proof when it arrives, then ask for more.

The argument for moving is simple. Wembley sold out for one final, so every women's league match should now be played in the biggest stadiums. The seats exist, the pitches exist, and the supporters have shown that they will come when they are invited properly.

Clubs will say that it costs to much to open a big ground. They said the same about floodlights, and about all-seater stands, and about every other improvement that turned out to pay for itself. Put the women's team on the main stage every week, and the crowds will follow.
