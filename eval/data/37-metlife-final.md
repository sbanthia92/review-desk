# The MetLife final was a verdict on parking the bus

I have read a dozen pieces since July telling me that the World Cup final was a dull game rescued by one goal. I watched it twice, and I think it was the most honest final in years. One team came to play football and the other came to survive, and for once the right one won.

The facts are stark. Spain had 65% of the ball and twenty shots. Argentina failed to register a single shot in regulation time, which had never happened in a World Cup final before. Emiliano Martínez made 9 saves, a record for a goalkeeper in a final, and for a long time it looked as though he would drag the game to penalties on his own. A crowd of 82,663 seen nothing but a siege.

Argentina's plan fell apart before the goal came. Enzo Fernández was sent off in the 82nd minute for a second yellow card, and a side that had barely crossed the halfway line now had ten men and thirty minutes of extra time to get through. Each of the Argentina midfielders were chasing shadows by then.

The goal, when it arrived, was simple. Ferran Torres scored in the 109th minute from a ball headed down by Nico Williams, and that was that. Spain had beaten France 2-0 in the semi-final in the same patient way, and they went into the final unbeaten in 37 matches. This was not luck. It was a method.

The clever combatant looks to the effect of combined energy, and does not require too much from individuals. Spain never asked one player to win the match. They asked eleven players to keep the ball until the other side broke, and it done exactly what it was designed to do.

Argentina's defenders will say that they nearly got away with it, and that the champions of 2022 are entitled to defend however they like. I do not accept that. A team with that much talent chose not to use it, and the sport is poorer every time such a choice is rewarded. Teams that have more than 60% of possession win four out of every five knockout matches at major tournaments. The numbers have been telling coaches this for years.

So here is the lesson I take from New Jersey. Spain won the final with 65% of the ball, so possession football is the only way to win a World Cup from now on. Coaches who still build there teams around a low block and a prayer are preparing for a game that no longer exists.

The half-time show got more headlines than the football, and the interval lasted 22 minutes while it went on. That tells you how the people who run the game see it. They think the product needs help. The second half of extra time at MetLife said otherwise: twenty-two players, one ball, and a team that deserved to win because it tried to.
