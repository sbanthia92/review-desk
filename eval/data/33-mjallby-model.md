# Mjällby are not a fairy tale, they are a warning

Every few years football finds a small club to feel good about, and in 2025 it found one on the south coast of Sweden. Mjällby AIF, from a fishing village that most Swedes could not place on a map, won the Allsvenskan for the first time. The story was told as a fairy tale. I think it should be read as something colder.

The facts first, because they are remarkable. Mjällby clinched the title on 20 October 2025 by winning 2-0 at IFK Göteborg, with two games to spare. They finished on 73 points, which is a league record, and they lost only once in thirty league matches. Hammarby were runners-up, nine points behind. Nobody had seen a season like it from a club of that size, and the coach, Anders Torstensson, became a national figure overnight.

It did not stop there. In May 2026 they beat Hammarby 2-1 in the cup final in Solna, in front of 29,903 people. Elliot Stroud scored after 19 minutes, Jacob Bergström got the winner before half-time, and a village club held both of Sweden's major trophies at once.

Here is where the fairy tale usually ends, and where my argument begin. Mjällby won the league on a small budget, so wage bills do not matter and the bigger clubs are simply wasting their money. I heard that sentence from pundits all winter, and I heard it from chairmen who wanted an excuse to spend less.

He will win who knows when to fight and when not to fight. Mjällby knew. They picked players that nobody else wanted and drilled them until the team was worth more than the sum of it's parts. But knowing when to fight is not the same as being able to fight every year.

Look at what happened next. Torstensson left at the end of the title season, and several key players followed him out of the door. In Europe, Mjällby lost 4-1 on aggregate to Slovan Bratislava in the third qualifying round of the Champions League. At home they slid towards the wrong end of the table, and in September 2026 the club sacked the Danish coach Karl Marius Aksum after a 4-1 defeat to IFK Göteborg, the same opponents they had beaten to become champions less than a year earlier. Andreas Brännström took over on 8 September 2026 with a contract to the end of the year.

That is the part the romantics leave out. Small clubs that win a title lose half of their squad within a year. The market notice everything, and a village club cannot say no to an offer that would pay its wages for a season.

So by all means enjoy Mjällby. I did. But do not tell me they prove that money has stopped mattering. They prove that a brilliant coach and a clever recruitment team can beat the odds once, and that the odds come back for their revenge as soon as the summer window opens.
