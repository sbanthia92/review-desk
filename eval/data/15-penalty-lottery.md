# Penalty shoot-outs are a lottery, so stop pretending otherwise

Every tournament ends the same way for somebody. A hundred and twenty minutes of football, then five kicks each, and a whole country decides that one unlucky player has let everybody down. I think it is time we were honest about what a shoot-out really is.

The penalty spot is ten yards from goal, and the goalkeeper has to move before he knows where the ball is going. From that distance a well-struck kick cannot be saved by skill, only by guessing. The World Cup's first shoot-out came in the 1982 semi-final, when Italy beat France in Seville, and nothing about the contest have changed since.

Look at who has suffered. Roberto Baggio missed the decisive kick in the 1994 final, and he had carried Italy to that final almost on his own. If it can happen to him, it can happen to anybody. All warfare is based on deception. The taker tries to fool the keeper, the keeper tries to fool the taker, and the winner is whoever guess right on the night.

The data says the same thing. Left-footed takers miss twice as often as right-footed ones in shoot-outs. Nobody can explain why, because there is nothing to explain. There is no pattern in who wins, only noise that we dress up as character afterwards.

That is why I lose patience with the talk of preparation. Shoot-outs are pure luck, so practising them is a waste of training time. Coaches who spends hours on penalties in the week before a knockout game are fooling themselves and their players. You would not practise tossing a coin, and you would not blame the coin when it came down tails.

So let us stop hounding the players who miss. They did not bottle it, and they did not lack courage. Your not watching a test of nerve when the kicks begin. You are watching a raffle with a very large audience, and somebody has to hold the losing ticket.

If we want a fairer ending, we should replay the match or keep playing until somebody scores. Until then, the least we can do is to stop treating the result as a verdict on anyone's character.
